China’s poverty alleviation strategy centered on the Targeted Poverty Alleviation Strategy, an approach closely associated with Xi Jinping and also situated within the national leadership context that included Li Keqiang. After the policy was first raised, it was officially adopted in 2014 and implemented through local governments, with the Chinese government and the Chinese Communist Party providing the broader governing framework.

Implementation emphasized targeted assistance rather than an undifferentiated approach to poverty. Local authorities were expected to merge poverty-alleviation resources, take targeted measures, and ensure that assistance reached poverty-stricken villages and households. These actions connected the national strategy to conditions of rural and absolute poverty at the local level.

Assessments of poverty could differ depending on whether World Bank criteria or China’s own poverty definition were applied. Within the Chinese framework, the strategy was linked to the declared elimination of absolute poverty, while continuing to focus on rural poverty and specifically identified villages and households.

Poverty alleviation was also tied to China’s wider development objectives. Addressing poverty was presented as part of building—and comprehensively building—a moderately prosperous society. It was further connected to the Communist Party’s century goal and the Four Comprehensives agenda, making targeted poverty reduction both a social policy and a component of the country’s broader political-development program.
