China’s poverty alleviation strategy centered on the Targeted Poverty Alleviation Strategy, closely associated with Xi Jinping and also situated within the national leadership of Xi and Li Keqiang. The Chinese Communist Party first raised the approach, and the Chinese government officially adopted it in 2014. Local governments then became central to its implementation, translating the broader strategy into targeted assistance.

Implementation involved merging poverty-alleviation resources, taking targeted measures, and ensuring that assistance reached poverty-stricken villages and households. The progression from adoption to local action linked national policy with conditions on the ground, including rural poverty and absolute poverty.

Assessments of poverty could differ depending on whether World Bank criteria or China’s own poverty definition were used. Within this framework, the strategy’s intended trajectory ran from identifying poor villages and households to eliminating absolute poverty.

Poverty alleviation was also connected to China’s wider development objectives. These included building—or comprehensively building—a moderately prosperous society, fulfilling a Party centenary goal, and advancing the Four Comprehensives agenda. The campaign therefore presented targeted assistance not only as a response to poverty but as part of a broader national development program.
