Sound recording may use either digital or analog techniques, and each approach has its own determinants of audio performance. In analog recording, bandwidth depends on the capabilities of the physical and electronic circuits. Analog performance is also shaped by noise sources such as flicker noise and imperfections in the recording medium. These factors affect dimensions including bandwidth, signal-to-noise ratio, linearity, noise level, and distortion level, while analog noise sources can produce both noise and broader sound distortion.

Digital recording is governed by a different set of constraints. Its bandwidth depends on the sample rate and the corresponding Nyquist frequency, while its signal-to-noise characteristics depend on bit depth and noise introduced by conversion circuits. Digital systems can also be affected by aliasing, artifacts from anti-aliasing filters, jitter, and quantization noise. Such mechanisms may result in recording errors, sound distortion, or noise.

Some audio characteristics are specific to the system being used rather than to a single general performance measure. These can include transparent filtering algorithms, harmonic saturation, and speed variation. In turn, such features may influence perceived or measured bandwidth, signal-to-noise ratio, linearity, noise, and distortion, contributing to the distinct behavior associated with analog and digital recording.
