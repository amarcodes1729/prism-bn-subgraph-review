Period poverty is a structural justice issue associated with both gender inequality and economic injustice. It encompasses inadequate access to sanitary products, menstrual-hygiene education, and essential infrastructure such as safe water, sanitation, washing facilities, and waste-management services. These conditions shape whether people can manage their periods safely and effectively.

Public understanding of period poverty can be influenced through social-media advocacy, consciousness raising, awareness campaigns, and arts advocacy. In turn, public perceptions are closely connected to policy responses, including period-poverty legislation. Such legislation is also framed by concerns about gender inequality and economic injustice and may address access to proper menstrual products, education in their effective use, and suitable hygiene infrastructure.

Safe period management depends not only on the availability of menstrual products but also on practical knowledge and adequate facilities. A lack of menstrual-hygiene education, products, washing facilities, waste management, safe water, or sanitation can contribute to unsafe management. Fear of shame and menstrual stigma can create additional barriers even where some resources are available.

Unsafe period management is linked to significant consequences in daily life and well-being. Attendance may suffer through missed school or work days. Physical-health outcomes can include infections and reproductive-tract complications, while psychological effects may include shame and other negative consequences. Addressing period poverty therefore involves more than distributing products: it also requires education, infrastructure, efforts against stigma, responsive legislation, and advocacy capable of changing public perceptions.
