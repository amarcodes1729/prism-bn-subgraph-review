The struggle over Scotland’s political status centered on competing territorial ambitions, military action, and diplomacy. English efforts to seize Scottish territory and claims of sovereignty over Scotland were opposed by Scottish attempts to keep English authority out of the country. These ambitions shaped a conflict that included English invasions of Scotland and Scottish resistance to English rule.

The English defeat at the Battle of Bannockburn was closely associated with Scotland’s de facto independence. Formal recognition, however, depended on diplomacy. The Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton was associated most strongly with de jure Scottish independence, distinguishing legal recognition from the independence achieved in practice through military success.

Other diplomatic alignments formed part of the wider contest, including the Scottish-French alliance of 1295 and a Flemish-English alliance. Together, these agreements, the opposing territorial claims, and the course of warfare influenced whether Scotland remained under English rule or secured independence in practice and in law.
