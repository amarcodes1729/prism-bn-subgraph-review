Official diplomatic publications known as “color books” are issued by major-power governments, particularly during wartime or other periods of crisis. Prominent examples include the British Blue Books, the German White Book, the Austrian Red Book, and the Russian Orange Book. These publications may address current events rather than past events and can be presented for educational or political reasons, to promote a government’s position, justify governmental action, or assign blame to foreign actors.

The publishing government shapes both the identity of the publication and the way its documents are curated. Governments may selectively include material, present it strategically, or arrange it in a calculated order. Such editorial choices, together with the geopolitical context and the stated rationale for publication, are associated with the books’ use as white propaganda or, more generally, government-issued propaganda. In that role, the publications provide an official account while supporting the issuing government’s position and directing blame toward foreign actors.
