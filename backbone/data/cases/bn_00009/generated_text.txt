The United States has a highly developed, diversified, and market-oriented economy. It has the world’s largest nominal gross domestic product, accounting for about 26 percent of global economic output, and the second-largest GDP measured by purchasing-power parity. Nominal and purchasing-power-adjusted GDP per capita are also high. This economic scale is associated with a large commercial banking system: commercial banks hold roughly $25 trillion in assets.

The economy’s scale rests partly on high productivity, rapid technological advancement, well-developed transportation infrastructure, and extensive natural resources. Innovation investment reinforces these strengths. The country ranks highly in global venture capital, provides substantial research and development funding, and spends about 3.46 percent of GDP on R&D, supporting cutting-edge work across multiple sectors.

These production inputs are reflected in a broad industrial base. The United States has the world’s second-largest manufacturing sector and produces about one-fifth of global manufacturing output. It also holds leading positions in petroleum, natural gas, and blood-products production. Together, its productive capacity and industrial output contribute to its overall economic scale.

Domestic demand is another defining feature. The United States is the world’s largest consumer market, with consumer spending representing more than 60 percent—and potentially as much as 70 percent—of the economy. This demand is closely connected both to the country’s economic size and to its trade orientation. The United States is the world’s largest importer and second-largest exporter, supported by free-trade agreements and major trading partnerships.

A favorable business environment accompanies the economy’s market orientation and diversification. The country ranks highly for ease of doing business and global competitiveness. This environment, combined with the economy’s sheer size, supports a substantial global corporate presence, including 139 of the world’s 500 largest companies.

Steady economic growth, low unemployment, and low inflation characterize its broader macroeconomic position. Along with economic scale, these conditions help underpin the international role of the U.S. dollar. The dollar is the foremost reserve currency, is widely used internationally, and serves as a reference standard in both petrodollar and eurodollar markets.

The dollar’s position is also associated with the depth of American public financial assets. The United States has a large Treasury securities market and the world’s largest gold reserves. These holdings, together with the country’s macroeconomic standing and economic scale, reinforce the central place of its currency in global finance.
