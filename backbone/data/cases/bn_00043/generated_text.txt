Native American reservations are sovereign territories managed by tribal governments, often in cooperation with the federal Bureau of Indian Affairs. Their governance arrangements are associated with varied reservation characteristics, including differences in physical size and population, as well as distinct political economies, cultures, and traditions.

Reservations range from large to small and from highly populated to sparsely populated. These characteristics help shape population distribution: some Native Americans are concentrated on the ten largest reservations, while others are dispersed across numerous other reservations. Distribution also differs according to whether people live on a reservation or reside off-reservation, reflecting the varied residential patterns of Native American populations.
