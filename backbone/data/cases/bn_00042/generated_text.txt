Poverty in Indonesia varies across the country’s islands and cultures. Its sprawling archipelago and the separation between islands are associated with differing impacts from one island to another. Conditions also vary among Java, Bali, Lombok, Sumatra, and New Guinea, while cultural and linguistic differences contribute to the uneven experience of isolation and hardship.

Isolation may be economic, cultural, linguistic, or physical. Papua and West Papua are particularly associated with these forms of isolation, which in turn are linked to Indonesia’s poverty patterns and to variation among islands and cultures. Although official poverty numbers may be declining, poverty remains widespread and serious.

The form poverty takes also differs by settlement. Dense rural areas are especially associated with rural poverty, while urban areas are associated with urban poverty. Major urban locations—including Jabodetabek, Medan, and Surabaya—form part of this urban pattern, illustrating how poverty is distributed differently across Indonesia’s metropolitan centers, rural communities, provinces, and geographically separated regions.
