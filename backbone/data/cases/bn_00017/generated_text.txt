Climate change, altered environmental conditions, and habitat change can reshape plant ecosystems through several connected pressures. These environmental alterations are associated with changes in plant function, shifts in geographic distribution, and changes in the diversity of native vegetation. They can also affect non-native plants and pests while altering wildfire patterns.

Wildfire regimes may respond through increased fire intensity, more severe burn conditions, or shorter intervals between burns. Such changes can leave native vegetation vulnerable or directly damaged. Non-native plants and pests likewise threaten native vegetation, adding another source of ecological stress.

Together, environmental alteration, biotic pressures, changing wildfire regimes, plant responses, and resulting vegetation harm are linked to declining plant biodiversity and broader biodiversity loss. Potential outcomes also include climate-related local extinction and threats to the biodiversity of native vegetation.
