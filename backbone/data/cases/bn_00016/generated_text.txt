Climate change scenarios and climate pathways are analytical constructs used to examine possible futures, from national and regional settings to the global scale. Models such as World3 and POLES can support this work, while assumptions about the number of people and population growth help define the futures under study. Other major driving forces include economic activity and the development of new technologies.

These scenarios and pathways connect scientific analysis with international climate governance. They inform processes associated with the Paris Agreement and the 2018 United Nations Climate Change Conference, alongside scientific inputs such as the IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5 °C and other scenario studies. They also contribute to policy-planning instruments, including adaptation plans and nationally determined contributions.

Within mitigation pathways, choices about the energy-supply portfolio and the use of carbon dioxide removal are closely associated with climate change mitigation, rather than adaptation. Mitigation and adaptation in turn relate to projected outcomes ranging from climate change and global warming generally to global warming of 1.5 °C. Those outcomes are also considered in relation to population conditions and the same underlying scenario drivers—population growth, economic activity, and new technologies—that shape climate scenarios and pathways in the first place.
