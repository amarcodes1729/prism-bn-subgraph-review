Climate-change analysis uses scenarios and pathways to explore possible futures at national, regional, and global scales. Modeling tools such as World3 and POLES help construct these analyses, while assumptions about the number of people, population growth, economic activity, and new technologies provide important driving conditions. These factors are also associated with the projected outcomes, including climate change, global warming, and the specific prospect of global warming reaching 1.5 °C.

Climate scenarios and pathways contribute to international governance processes such as the Paris Agreement and the 2018 United Nations Climate Change Conference. Scientific inputs—including scenario studies and the IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5 °C—also inform these processes. At the policy-planning level, scenario analysis supports the development of adaptation plans and nationally determined contributions.

Pathways for responding to climate change may incorporate different energy-supply portfolios and carbon dioxide removal. These components are linked especially closely with climate-change mitigation, while adaptation provides another major response strategy. The balance between mitigation and adaptation, together with demographic and economic or technological assumptions, shapes how analysts consider broad climate change, global warming, and pathways consistent with limiting warming to 1.5 °C.
